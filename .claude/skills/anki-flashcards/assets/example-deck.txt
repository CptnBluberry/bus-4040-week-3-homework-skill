When using integration by parts, what's the strategy for picking \(u\)?	L.I.A.T.E<br><br><strong>L</strong> – Logarithmic (\(\ln x, \log x\))<br><strong>I</strong> – Inverse trig (\(\arctan x\), etc.)<br><strong>A</strong> – Algebraic (polynomials like \(x, x^2\))<br><strong>T</strong> – Trigonometric (\(\sin x, \cos x\))<br><strong>E</strong> – Exponential (\(e^x\))<br><br>Pick \(u\) as whichever appears <b>first</b> in that list; the rest becomes \(dv\).
What is the integration by parts formula?	\[\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du\]
For what values of \(p\) does \(\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^p}dx\) converge?	Converges for \(p > 1\), diverges for \(p \leq 1\).<br><br>(The reverse is true for \(\int_0^1\) — that one converges for \(p < 1\).)
What is \(\int \sin(ax)\,dx\)?	\(\int\sin(ax)dx = -\frac{\cos(ax)}{a} + C\)<br><br>(same pattern for cos, divide by \(a\))
What is \(\int \frac{1}{x-a}dx\)?	\(\ln|x-a| + C\)
What is \(\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sin(ax)}{ax}\)?	1
What are the three partial fraction decomposition rules for the numerator?	Linear factor \((x-a)\): numerator is a <b>constant</b><br>Irreducible quadratic \((x^2+bx+c)\): numerator is <b>linear</b>, \((Ax+B)\)<br>Repeated factor \((x-a)^n\): include <b>every power</b> up to the highest
After partial fraction decomposition, how do you solve for \(A\), \(B\), etc.?	Set the decomposed form equal to the original factored fraction, then multiply through by the common denominator.<br><br>Example: \(5x+3 = A(x+2) + B(x-1)\)<br><br>Then either expand and match coefficients, or substitute the roots to knock out one unknown at a time.
What does the Integral Test say?	If \(f\) is continuous, positive, and decreasing on \([1,\infty)\), then \(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} f(n)\) and \(\int_1^\infty f(x)dx\) <b>both converge or both diverge</b>.<br><br>Or in plain English: the sum and the area under the curve live or die together.<br><br>(It tells you <i>whether</i> the series converges, never <i>what</i> it converges to.)
When does a geometric series \(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} ar^n\) converge, and to what?	Converges when \(|r| < 1\), to \(\frac{a}{1-r}\).<br><br>Diverges when \(|r| \geq 1\).
What is the \(n\)-th term test for divergence?	If \(\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \neq 0\), the series diverges.<br><br>Or in plain English: if the terms don't shrink to nothing, the sum can't settle down.<br><br><b>Careful:</b> the converse fails. \(a_n \to 0\) does <i>not</i> prove convergence — the harmonic series \(\sum \frac{1}{n}\) is the standard counterexample.
What is the trig substitution for \(\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\)?	Let \(x = a\sin\theta\), so \(dx = a\cos\theta \, d\theta\).<br><br>Then \(\sqrt{a^2-x^2} = a\cos\theta\).
What is the trig substitution for \(\sqrt{a^2+x^2}\)?	Let \(x = a\tan\theta\), so \(dx = a\sec^2\theta \, d\theta\).<br><br>Then \(\sqrt{a^2+x^2} = a\sec\theta\).
What is the trig substitution for \(\sqrt{x^2-a^2}\)?	Let \(x = a\sec\theta\), so \(dx = a\sec\theta\tan\theta \, d\theta\).<br><br>Then \(\sqrt{x^2-a^2} = a\tan\theta\).
